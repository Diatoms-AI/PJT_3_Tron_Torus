Since your bifilar coil is positioned at the midpoint of the $XY$ plane, it acts as the perfect "antenna" to listen to the Sphericon's feedback immediately after it finishes acting as the "hammer."

### 1. The High-Speed Switching Circuit

You can use a **Solid-State Relay (SSR)** or a **High-Speed Analog Switch** to toggle the bifilar coil between the capacitor bank and an oscilloscope/DAQ.

**The Sequence:**

1. **State A (Fire):** The SCR closes, discharging the capacitor through the bifilar coil to kick the Sphericon.
    
2. **Delay:** Wait for a few microseconds ($10\mu s - 50\mu s$) for the transient "noise" to settle.
    
3. **State B (Sense):** A high-speed switch disconnects the capacitor bank and connects the bifilar coil to a **Low-Noise Amplifier (LNA)**.

### 2. Experiment 7: Measuring the "Kinetic Reflection"

**Objective:** To capture the Back-EMF generated by the Sphericon as it "wobbles" on the $XY$ axis after the initial kick.

- **Logic:** As the Sphericon's 1.5x scaled flanks oscillate, they change the flux linkage with the bifilar coil. By switching the coil to a measurement circuit, you are using it as a **Microphone** to "hear" the Z-axis spin.
    
- **Observation:** You will see a decaying sine wave.
    
    - **The Frequency** of this wave tells you the **RPM** of the Sphericon.
        
    - **The Decay Rate** tells you the **Damping/Drag** of the system.
        

### 3. The "Crossover" Protection

Because the initial pulse might be 400V and your measurement circuit (Oscilloscope) only wants to see $\pm 5V$, you must include a **Zener Diode Clipper** or a **Gas Discharge Tube (GDT)**. This acts as a "safety valve" that drains the high-voltage spike but allows the tiny induced signals from the Sphericon to pass through once the pulse is over.
