To calculate the torque, we must treat the Sphericon as a **variable reluctance rotor** being hit by a transverse magnetic "shear" from the bifilar coil. Because the Sphericon is scaled $1.5\times$ on the $Z$-axis, it acts as a magnetic lever.

### The Torque Equation for the Sphericon

The torque ($\tau$) produced by a single pulse is a function of the change in **System Inductance ($L$)** relative to the **Rotation Angle ($\theta$)** of the Sphericon:

$$\tau = \frac{1}{2} I^2 \frac{dL}{d\theta}$$

In your specific Limaçon geometry, the "Pulse Circuit" creates a non-linear $dL/d\theta$ because the $XY$ flanks of the Sphericon pass the "horns" of the loops at high velocity.

---

### 1. Estimating the Magnetic Force ($F$)

Before we get to torque, we calculate the instantaneous force ($F$) exerted by the "Magnetic Pressure" ($P_m$) against the surface area ($A$) of the Sphericon's flank:

1. **Magnetic Pressure:** $P_m = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$
    
2. **Surface Area ($A$):** For your 1.5x scaled Sphericon, the flank area is approximately $A \approx 2 \times (\text{base\_radius} \times \text{height})$.
    
3. **Force:** $F = P_m \times A$
    

### 2. The Torque Calculation Script (Python/Pseudocode)

You can use this logic to estimate the "kick" the Z-axis receives from a single bifilar pulse.

Python

```
import math

# Constants
mu_0 = 4 * math.pi * 1e-7  # Permeability of free space
radius_horn = 0.02         # 20mm in meters
bifilar_current = 50       # Amps (Peak Pulse)
num_loops = 11

def calculate_torque(current, b_field_peak):
    # 1. Calculate Magnetic Pressure at the Horn
    # Assuming the geometry concentrates B-field by 4x at the horn
    b_at_horn = b_field_peak * 4 
    pressure = (b_at_horn**2) / (2 * mu_0)
    
    # 2. Area of the Sphericon Flank (1.5x scale)
    # Area in m^2 for a base_radius of 12mm
    area_flank = 0.012 * 0.018 # width * height
    
    # 3. Tangential Force (Simplified Lorentz/Reluctance)
    force = pressure * area_flank
    
    # 4. Torque at Z-axis (Force * Radius)
    torque = force * radius_horn
    return torque

# Example result
peak_b = 0.05 # 500 Gauss from bifilar pulse
t = calculate_torque(bifilar_current, peak_b)
print(f"Estimated Instantaneous Torque: {t:.4f} Nm")
```
