### 1. The "Static Twist" Fallacy

In a DC setup, magnetic field lines around a conductor always form closed loops. While the **physical shape** of your loops is a Limaçon, the **magnetic field vectors** at any given point are the result of linear superposition.

- **The Problem:** Without a time-varying component (AC) or a specific phase offset between the 11 loops, you will likely measure a complex "clumping" of flux, but not a **dynamic vortex**.
    
- **Success Metric Risk:** You expect a tangential "twist." In a DC system, the field lines will simply follow the shortest path to complete their loop; they won't "corkscrew" unless there is a helical current path or a rotating magnetic field.
    

### 2. The "Horn" Saturation & Heat
